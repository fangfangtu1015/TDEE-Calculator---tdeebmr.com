Accuracy of Calculations

While we strive to use the most accurate and well-established scientific formulas available, all calculations provided by TDEEBMR are estimates based on population-level predictive equations. Individual results can and do vary significantly from calculated values due to factors including but not limited to genetics, hormonal status, body composition, metabolic health, medication use, and measurement accuracy.

The formulas we use (Mifflin-St Jeor, Harris-Benedict, Katch-McArdle, and others) have documented accuracy rates that typically fall within 10% of measured values for approximately 70-90% of the population, depending on the formula and individual characteristics. This means your actual metabolic rate may differ from our estimates by 200-400 calories per day or more.

We do not guarantee the accuracy, completeness, or reliability of any calculations, estimates, or information provided on this website. Users should treat all results as approximate starting points for discussion with a qualified healthcare professional.

Special Populations Warning

Important Notice

The following individuals should not use TDEEBMR tools without direct supervision and approval from a qualified healthcare professional:

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women: Nutritional needs during pregnancy and lactation are significantly different from standard calculations and require specialized medical guidance.
  • Children and adolescents under 18: Growing bodies have unique nutritional requirements that are not accurately represented by adult predictive equations. Pediatric nutritional guidance should come from a qualified pediatrician or pediatric dietitian.
  • Individuals with eating disorders: Calorie counting, weight tracking, and body composition focus can be triggering or harmful for individuals with current or past eating disorders, including anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, and other specified feeding or eating disorders (OSFED). If you struggle with disordered eating, please contact the National Eating Disorders Association (NEDA) helpline or a qualified mental health professional.
  • Individuals with medical conditions: People with diabetes, thyroid disorders, kidney disease, liver disease, heart conditions, cancer, or other metabolic or chronic illnesses may have nutritional needs that differ substantially from standard calculations. Always consult your treating physician before making dietary changes.
  • Elderly individuals (over 65): Age-related changes in metabolism, body composition, and nutritional needs may make standard equations less accurate. Geriatric nutritional guidance should be personalized by a healthcare professional.
  • Individuals taking medications that affect metabolism: Certain medications, including corticosteroids, beta-blockers, antidepressants, and anticonvulsants, can significantly alter metabolic rate and nutritional requirements.
