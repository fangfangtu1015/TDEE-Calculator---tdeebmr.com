Understanding Body Fat Percentage

Body fat percentage represents the proportion of your total body weight that is composed of adipose (fat) tissue. Unlike BMI, which only considers total weight relative to height, body fat percentage directly measures what matters most for health and aesthetics: the ratio of fat mass to lean mass.

There are two types of body fat: subcutaneous fat (stored under the skin) and visceral fat (stored around internal organs). Visceral fat is more metabolically active and strongly linked to cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and inflammation. Two people with identical body fat percentages can have very different health profiles based on where that fat is distributed.

The US Navy Circumference Method

The U.S. Navy body fat formula was developed by Hodgdon and Beckett (1984) at the Naval Health Research Center in San Diego. It uses circumference measurements to estimate body density, which is then converted to body fat percentage using the Siri equation (body fat % = (495 / body density) - 450).

US Navy Formula

Men: Body Fat % = 86.010 x log10(waist - neck) - 70.041 x log10(height) + 36.76
Women: Body Fat % = 163.205 x log10(waist + hip - neck) - 97.684 x log10(height) - 78.387

These published constants are defined for measurements in inches (Hodgdon & Beckett, 1984). The calculator converts centimetre inputs to inches before applying the equation, so both unit modes use the same formula.

The Navy method has been validated against hydrostatic weighing and shows a correlation of approximately r = 0.7-0.8 with DEXA scans. Its standard error of estimate is approximately 3-4% body fat, making it suitable for tracking trends over time but less precise than clinical methods.

How to Measure Correctly

Accurate measurements are critical for reliable results. Follow these guidelines:

  • Neck: Measure at the narrowest point, just below the Adam's apple. Keep the tape level and snug but not compressing the skin. Look straight ahead during measurement.
  • Waist (men): Measure horizontally at the navel (umbilicus). Measure at the end of a normal exhale, not while sucking in.
  • Waist (women): Measure at the narrowest point of the natural waistline, typically just above the navel.
  • Hips (women only): Measure at the widest point of the buttocks/hips.
  • Height: Measure without shoes.

For consistency, measure at the same time of day (morning, fasted, before exercise), use the same tape measure, and take 2-3 measurements at each site to average.

Key Takeaway

The US Navy method is a practical, free tool for tracking body fat trends over time. For the most accurate single measurement, use DEXA. For consistent tracking, measure circumference at the same time each week and use this calculator. Remember that body fat percentage is more meaningful than total weight for assessing body composition progress.

Frequently Asked Questions

The US Navy circumference method has a correlation of approximately r=0.7-0.8 with DEXA scans (the gold standard), with a standard error of about 3-4% body fat. It is more accurate for men than women due to different fat distribution patterns. The method was developed by Hodgdon and Beckett (1984) at the Naval Health Research Center. For the most accurate results, measure at the same time of day (morning is best), use a flexible tape measure, and take 2-3 measurements at each site to average.

According to the American Council on Exercise (ACE), healthy body fat ranges are: For men - Essential fat: 2-5%, Athletes: 6-13%, Fitness: 14-17%, Average: 18-24%, Above Average: 25%+. For women - Essential fat: 10-13%, Athletes: 14-20%, Fitness: 21-24%, Average: 25-31%, Above Average: 32%+. Women naturally carry more body fat due to reproductive requirements. Body fat below essential levels is dangerous and can cause hormonal dysfunction.

For men: measure neck circumference at the narrowest point below the Adam's apple, and waist circumference at the navel. For women: measure neck as for men, waist at the narrowest point (natural waistline), and hips at the widest point. Use a flexible tape measure, keep it snug but not compressing the skin, and take measurements on bare skin. Measure in the morning before eating for consistency.

DEXA (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry) is the gold standard for body composition analysis, providing precise measurements of fat mass, lean mass, and bone density with approximately 1-2% error. Hydrostatic (underwater) weighing is also highly accurate (~2% error). Bod Pod (air displacement plethysmography) offers similar accuracy to hydrostatic weighing. Skinfold calipers (3-7 site measurements) can be accurate (3-4% error) when performed by an experienced technician. Bioelectrical impedance (BIA) scales are convenient but less accurate, with errors of 4-8%.

Gym and home body fat scales use bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), which sends a small electrical current through the body and estimates body fat based on the resistance encountered. BIA accuracy is heavily influenced by hydration status, recent food intake, exercise, skin temperature, and even the time of day. Studies show BIA can vary by 4-8% compared to DEXA depending on conditions. For tracking trends, use the same scale at the same time of day under consistent conditions, or use circumference measurements with the Navy formula.
